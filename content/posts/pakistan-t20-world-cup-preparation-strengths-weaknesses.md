Pakistan's early exit from the 2024 T20 World Cup in the West Indies was the kind of result that demanded reflection, not just reaction. A side that once set the template for T20 cricket now finds itself in an uncomfortable conversation about identity, selection, and whether the men in charge have a coherent plan for the next edition of the tournament.

## The Real Question Is Not Talent, It Is Structure

Pakistan has never lacked for talent. The concern, as it has been for the better part of a decade, is whether the selection system translates that talent into a settled, purposeful eleven. The squad that goes into the next T20 World Cup will be shaped significantly by what happens in the PSL, home series, and tours over the coming months. The selectors have a window. The question is whether they use it wisely or lapse into the familiar habits of reactive tinkering.

## Where Pakistan Is Strong

The bowling attack, on its day, remains one of the most dangerous in world cricket. Shaheen Shah Afridi, when fit and motivated, can unsettle any top order inside the powerplay. Haris Rauf's pace and Naseem Shah's ability to generate lift on unhelpful surfaces give Pakistan a three-pronged seam threat that few nations can match.

In the batting department, Babar Azam's quality is not seriously in question even if his strike rate in T20Is continues to attract scrutiny. He remains Pakistan's most technically sound batter and, when the conditions suit his style, a match-winner at the top. Mohammad Rizwan's reliability as an accumulator who can shift gears provides a stable platform.

The leg-spin option through Shadab Khan, when he is among the wickets and not just filling an over, adds a genuine match-winning dimension. Pakistan's best T20 XI on paper is competitive with any side in the world.

## Where the Problems Are Buried

The middle order is where Pakistan's T20 ambitions have repeatedly come unstuck. The numbers four through six remain alarmingly unsettled. Too often, Pakistan have entered knockout stages of tournaments without a clear idea of who bats at five in a chase, or who provides the final-over hitting at six and seven. That is not a personnel problem alone. It is a strategic failure.

The powerplay batting approach is also overdue for a frank internal conversation. Relying on Babar and Rizwan to do the heavy lifting at the top means that when either falls cheaply, the innings rarely recovers with sufficient authority. Pakistan need a batter willing to play aerial cricket from ball one. That profile has been discussed for years and remains unfilled with any consistency.

In bowling, the death-over economy has been a recurring vulnerability. Pakistan concede too many boundaries between overs sixteen and twenty, partly because Shaheen's best work is in the powerplay and partly because the support options lack variety. A reliable wrist-spinner or an additional yorker specialist would reduce the team's dependence on conditions.

Fitness and availability are also structural concerns. The injury management of front-line seamers has improved but is still not at the standard of the top Test nations. Losing Naseem Shah or Shaheen to injury mid-tournament is not a hypothetical. It has happened before.

## What This Means Going Forward

The next T20 World Cup gives Pakistan time to address these problems, but only if the selection and coaching setup makes deliberate choices rather than waiting for a crisis to force their hand. The PSL is the most immediate laboratory. Selectors should be using it to observe middle-order candidates under pressure, identify death bowlers, and give young T20 specialists enough exposure to be genuinely considered rather than called up as afterthoughts.

Babar Azam's captaincy and his relationship with the coaching staff will also shape the squad's readiness. A settled captain who has the trust of management and players tends to produce settled team selection. That environment has not always existed for Pakistan in recent cycles.

## What to Watch Next

The upcoming home T20I series and Pakistan's PSL campaign provide the clearest signals. Watch whether selectors back the same middle-order combination across matches or revert to shuffling the pack after every failure. Watch whether a genuine powerplay aggressor emerges at the top. And watch whether the management publicly commits to a batting philosophy, or whether the old ambiguity returns.

Pakistan have the pieces. Building something coherent from them is a different challenge entirely. The next T20 World Cup is not a pipe dream. It is, however, a long way from a plan.
