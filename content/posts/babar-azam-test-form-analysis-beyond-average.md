Babar Azam remains the most scrutinised batter in Pakistan cricket, and rightly so. When he struggles in Tests, the middle order wobbles. When the middle order wobbles, Pakistan lose. The stakes of his form could not be higher.

## The Number That Flatters, and the Numbers That Do Not

A career Test average hovering around 45 is the first thing Babar's defenders reach for, and it is not nothing. Among Pakistan batters since 2010, only a handful have sustained that kind of return across formats. But averages are a blunt instrument. They compress context, flatten variance, and hide the thing that actually concerns selectors and coaches right now: the pattern of how Babar is getting out, and when.

Strip away the big scores from subcontinental conditions against modest attacks, and a leaner picture emerges from his recent campaigns. In away Tests over the last two years, Babar's conversion rate from fifty to hundred has dipped noticeably. He reaches the thirties and forties with a fluency that still marks him as class. Then, with uncomfortable regularity, he departs. The off stump channel, the nip-backer from over the wicket, the fuller delivery angled into the pads on seaming surfaces: these have been recurring dismissal triggers, and opposition analysts have clearly taken note.

## The Angle: A Batter in a Technical Conversation With Himself

This is less a crisis of ability and more a crisis of method. Babar has always been a back-and-across batter. Against high-quality seam and swing in helpful conditions, that trigger movement can leave him marginally vulnerable to the straight ball, particularly when bowlers are disciplined enough to hold a fuller length. England's seamers exposed this thread during their tours. South Africa's pace attack pulled at it. The pattern is repeating often enough that it cannot be dismissed as bad luck or poor scheduling.

There is also the question of intent. Babar's red-ball game, at its most effective, is built on accumulation and control. He is not a Bazball batter and never claimed to be. But the risk-aversion that served him brilliantly in an earlier phase of his career can, when form dips even slightly, slide into passivity. He can look stuck. And a stuck Babar is exactly what a disciplined bowling attack wants to see.

None of this diminishes the quality that got him to the top. But quality, unexamined, does not maintain itself.

## What It Means Going Forward

For Pakistan's Test fortunes, Babar's output at number three is non-negotiable. The batting order around him remains in flux. Imam-ul-Haq and Abdullah Shafique have had inconsistent periods of their own. Saud Shakeel has shown promise but needs partnership support to build innings. If Babar is converting fifties into hundreds at his peak rate, Pakistan can post competitive totals. If he is not, the team's fragility in away conditions is ruthlessly exposed.

The technical corrections, if they are being worked on, tend to happen quietly in nets and one-on-one sessions with the coaching staff. There is no public signal yet that the management views this as an acute problem rather than a temporary one. That may be the right call. Tinkering publicly with a batter of Babar's standing carries its own risks. But the private work needs to be happening.

At the international level, opponents share video. The file on Babar's dismissal patterns is being updated after every series. Pakistan's coaching group cannot afford to be the last team in the room to read it.

## What to Watch Next

The next Test assignment will tell a great deal. Watch specifically for how Babar responds in his first innings of each match, particularly if Pakistan are under early pressure. A batter working through a technical adjustment often shows it most clearly under stress, not during smooth runs. Look at his foot movement to the fuller ball. Watch whether he is presenting a straighter bat on the off side or whether the hard hands that have cost him in recent years remain a feature.

Watch also for where he scores his runs. A Babar functioning well accumulates through cover and mid-off with elegant timing. A Babar in partial difficulty deflects finer and takes more edges through third man. The wagon wheel does not lie.

Pakistan cricket needs Babar Azam at his best in the longest format. That is not sentiment. It is arithmetic. The question is whether the batter and the system around him are treating the current moment with the seriousness it demands.
