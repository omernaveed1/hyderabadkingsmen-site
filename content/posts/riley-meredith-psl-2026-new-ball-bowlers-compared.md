Riley Meredith has never been a bowler you ease into. He arrives at the crease like a runaway freight train, and the PSL 2026 auction rooms clearly factored that in. But pace alone does not win T20 powerplays, and several other franchises have assembled new-ball arsenals that deserve equal scrutiny.

## The Real Question Here

PSL 2026 is shaping up as a tournament where the powerplay battle could define outcomes more than any middle-overs fireworks. Batting lineups across the eight franchises have deepened considerably. The question is not simply who bowls fastest, but who can combine pace, movement, and tactical variety inside the first six overs on Pakistani pitches, where the ball does enough off the seam to reward skill but rarely enough to excuse wayward lines.

Meredith, the Queensland and Big Bash enforcer, is the most compelling pure pace option in the overseas pool. His radar can wobble, but when it locks in, he generates steep bounce and genuine throat-ball threat from a low, slingy action. That combination is rare. On the flat surfaces of Lahore and Karachi, rare and uncomfortable can be the difference between a powerplay score of 45 for one and 70 for none.

## Comparing the Field

The obvious benchmark is Shaheen Shah Afridi. Whatever franchise secures Shaheen for the new ball gets a left-armer who swings it both ways, reads conditions intelligently, and has the temperament of someone who has taken wickets in World Cup finals. His injury history remains the asterisk, but a fit Shaheen opening the bowling in PSL conditions is the gold standard, full stop.

Hasan Ali, when his back is cooperative, still generates sharp off-cutters and an awkward angle for right-handers. He is not quite the enforcer he once was, but his wicket-taking instincts at the top of an innings are underrated. Franchises that have written him off on form alone may find themselves revisiting that view mid-tournament.

Naseem Shah sits in a different category entirely. Still only in his early twenties, Naseem brings a combination of outswing and genuine pace that few bowlers his age can match globally. His PSL record under pressure has been inconsistent, but his ceiling remains higher than almost any Pakistani pacer currently active. If he is fully match-fit and carrying rhythm from whatever international cricket precedes the tournament, he is arguably the most dangerous new-ball option in the entire competition.

From the overseas contingent, Meredith's closest rival in pure pace terms is likely to be whoever fills the aggressive Australian-style seamer slot across other franchises. Names like Lockie Ferguson, if available from the IPL window, or other high-velocity operators from the Caribbean and South Africa bring similar short-ball threat with arguably better T20 economy discipline than Meredith has shown across his career.

## What Meredith Offers That Others Do Not

The honest case for Meredith is specificity. He does one thing at an extremely high level: he bowls very fast, with a scrambled-seam or back-of-a-length delivery that sits up into the body and hurries even accomplished batters. In a tournament where top-order batters have faced everything and are largely untroubled by movement, raw pace remains the disruptor.

His economy in the Big Bash has not always been pristine, but the PSL pitches and their relatively less-receptive outfields can actually benefit a bowler who hits the pitch hard. The ball does not skid through as freely, and steep bounce becomes a greater weapon.

The risk is his control in death overs, where his wayward spells have cost teams dearly in previous T20 competitions. Most franchises would be wise to use him as a powerplay specialist and protect him from the final two overs unless conditions firmly dictate otherwise.

## What to Watch Going Forward

Watch how franchises deploy their new-ball pairings in the first three matches of PSL 2026. Early tactical decisions, whether a team pairs Meredith with a swing bowler to maximize both disciplines, or whether Shaheen and Naseem are trusted to carry the entire opening burst, will reveal coaching philosophies.

Also watch the toss patterns. Pitches that have dew in the evening session reward pace bowling early. Franchises who win tosses and choose to bowl first in those conditions will likely expose their most dangerous new-ball options immediately, and that is when this comparative question gets answered in real time rather than on paper.

Meredith is a genuine threat. He is not the automatic number one. The PSL 2026 new-ball picture is more competitive than it has been in years, and that is very good news for the tournament.
