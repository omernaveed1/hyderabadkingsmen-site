PSL 2026 is done and dusted, and the debates have already started. Who built well, who wasted their resources, and who got lucky? Ranking all eight franchises forces honest answers to those questions, and the answers matter because this league is Pakistan's most important talent pipeline.

## The Angle

Every PSL season is really two competitions running simultaneously: the trophy race, and the longer game of squad-building, emerging talent, and franchise direction. A team can finish third and still win the second competition. Equally, a team can reach the final on the back of three overseas stars and leave Pakistan cricket no richer for the experience. That double lens is what separates a useful retrospective from a simple points-table reprint.

---

## 8. Multan Sultans

The defending champions arrived with expectation and departed with questions. Their middle order looked brittle whenever the overseas batting firepower was absent, and the bowling attack lacked variety in the back half of innings. A group-stage exit for a franchise of their resources counts as failure, plain and simple. The bigger concern is a lack of identifiable young Pakistani pace talent coming through the ranks.

## 7. Peshawar Zalmi

Zalmi are Pakistan cricket's most sentimental franchise, always capable of conjuring moments that feel bigger than the match. In 2026 they conjured some good ones, but not enough of them. Their top order was inconsistent, their spin options thin on turning tracks, and they fell just short of the play-offs. The talent is there in scattered form. Someone needs to connect the dots.

## 6. Karachi Kings

Karachi finished sixth and made it feel like progress, which tells you something about where this franchise has been. The bowling attack showed genuine improvement, and a couple of locally developed pace bowlers gave supporters something to file away for the future. But the batting remained too reliant on one or two names, and when those names failed, the innings folded quickly. Steady progress, but the Kings are still a couple of key decisions away from genuine contention.

## 5. Hyderabad Kingsmen

The Kingsmen sit in the middle of this table and it is a fair reflection of a campaign that was equal parts promise and frustration. Their batting depth was the most encouraging feature, with at least three Pakistani batters averaging above 30 in the group stage. The bowling, particularly in the powerplay, leaked too many runs. Head coach decisions in key moments also drew scrutiny. A play-off spot was within reach and they did not take it. That gap between potential and result is what the franchise needs to close in the off-season.

## 4. Islamabad United

United's campaign was organised, professional, and slightly cold. They qualified comfortably, managed their squad rotations well, and then ran out of steam in the knock-out rounds. The old complaint about United applies again: they are very good at being very good, without quite finding that extra gear when the stakes are highest. Still, several young Islamabad-contracted players pushed their case for national selection, and that is not nothing.

## 3. Lahore Qalandars

Lahore's academy pipeline remains the envy of the league, and it showed again. Two teenagers featured regularly and neither looked out of place. The Qalandars reached the final and pushed the champions hard before falling short in a tight finish. Their willingness to trust youth while remaining competitive at the top end is a model other franchises should study more seriously.

## 2. Quetta Gladiators

Quetta were the season's most complete bowling side. Their pace attack was relentless and their captain read the game sharply throughout. A semi-final exit felt harsh given their consistency across 10 group matches. The batting order, top-heavy and a little fragile below four, is the one thing preventing this from being ranked first. Fix that and the Gladiators will be title favourites next year.

## What to Watch Next

The PSL 2026 player draft window is the next big milestone. Which franchises address their clear weaknesses, and which repeat the same mistakes? For the Kingsmen specifically, the powerplay bowling question needs a structural answer, not just a personnel one. For the national selectors watching all eight campaigns, the league has again thrown up names worth monitoring ahead of Pakistan's upcoming bilateral commitments.

This league works best when it creates genuine national team options. By that measure, PSL 2026 did its job, imperfectly but unmistakably.
