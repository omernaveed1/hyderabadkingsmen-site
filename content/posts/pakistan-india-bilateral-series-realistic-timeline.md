The question surfaces every few months like clockwork: when will Pakistan and India play bilateral cricket again? After the two sides met at the Champions Trophy in February 2025, the clamor has grown louder. But wanting something and being able to schedule it are very different things, and anyone selling you a firm date is probably selling you something else too.

## The Real Issue Is Not Cricket

Strip away the fans, the broadcasters, and the BCCI's vast balance sheet, and what remains is a political problem. Pakistan and India have not played a bilateral series since 2012-13, when India toured Pakistan for a limited-overs rubber. Every conversation since has stalled at the same wall: the Indian government's reluctance to issue No Objection Certificates for tours to Pakistan, and the BCCI's corresponding inability, or unwillingness, to push back against that position.

The Champions Trophy in February 2025 offered a temporary workaround. India played their matches at a neutral venue, Pakistan stayed home, and the ICC held the whole arrangement together with diplomatic tape. It worked, just about. But a neutral-venue compromise for a bilateral series is a logical contradiction. The entire commercial and emotional value of a Pakistan-India series lies in the home-and-away dynamic, in Lahore and Karachi on one side, Mumbai and Chennai on the other. A series played entirely in a third country is not a bilateral series in any meaningful sense.

## The Money Argument Cuts Both Ways

Broadcasters and sponsors often argue that financial pressure will eventually force the two boards together. The logic is straightforward: a Pakistan-India bilateral series would generate revenues that dwarf almost any other cricket event outside the IPL and ICC tournaments. The PCB, perpetually stretched, would benefit enormously. So, in theory, would the BCCI.

But the BCCI does not need Pakistan cricket the way Pakistan cricket needs the BCCI. India's domestic market is so large, and the IPL so dominant, that bilateral series with Pakistan represent a marginal gain for them and a transformative one for Pakistan. That asymmetry gives India almost all the negotiating leverage, and they have shown little urgency in using it. When one side stands to gain far more than the other, the more comfortable party simply waits.

## What Would Actually Need to Change

For a genuine bilateral series to happen, three things need to align simultaneously. First, a thaw in diplomatic relations between the two governments sufficient to produce the necessary clearances. Second, the BCCI's willingness to advocate for a tour rather than simply defer to government signaling. Third, an ICC or international body willing to apply meaningful pressure, perhaps through Future Tours Programme obligations, rather than offering neutral-venue escapes every time.

None of those three conditions is close to being met right now. The diplomatic climate between Islamabad and New Delhi is, at best, cautious. The BCCI has shown no appetite for leading on this issue. And the ICC has consistently prioritized the financial stability that India's participation brings over any principled stand on tour obligations.

The most optimistic scenario involves a sustained improvement in diplomatic relations over the next two to three years, followed by a limited-overs series perhaps in a neutral country initially, with a full home-and-away programme following a cycle or two later. Call it 2028 at the earliest for something resembling a proper bilateral series, and that estimate requires considerable goodwill on both sides.

## What Pakistan Cricket Should Focus On Instead

For the PCB and Pakistan's selectors, the bilateral question is ultimately a distraction from more immediately solvable problems. Pakistan's Test side is in a state of transition. The batting order has been chronically inconsistent. The red-ball pipeline needs serious attention. These are problems that Pakistan can address regardless of what New Delhi decides.

The PSL continues to grow as a commercial and cricketing product. The Hyderabad Kingsmen franchise has added a new market and new eyeballs to the league. Pakistan's white-ball teams have shown genuine competitiveness in ICC events. There is plenty of cricket to play, and plenty of work to do.

## What to Watch Next

The most significant near-term indicator will be whether Pakistan and India engage in any bilateral government-level talks that include cricket on the agenda. Watch also for any ICC board meetings where Future Tours Programme compliance becomes a formal agenda item. If the BCCI begins to face procedural pressure over non-fulfilment of bilateral obligations, the conversation changes character entirely.

Until then, the Pakistan-India bilateral series remains what it has been for over a decade: the most discussed match in cricket that nobody actually schedules.
