## The Fixture the Whole Cricket World Wants

A Pakistan-India bilateral series has not taken place on either country's soil since 2012-13. That is more than a decade of the sport's greatest rivalry reduced to neutral-venue ICC events and the occasional Asia Cup scramble. For Pakistan cricket, the financial and psychological stakes of restoring that fixture could not be higher.

But let us be clear about what kind of story this actually is. It is not, at its heart, a cricket story. It is a geopolitics story dressed in whites.

## What Is Really Going On

The two boards, the PCB and the BCCI, exist in a peculiar condition. Their administrators maintain civil working relationships inside ICC meetings. They vote on the same playing conditions, share broadcast revenues from ICC events, and occasionally exchange pleasantries at Colombo or Dubai hotel lobbies. Yet the moment a bilateral series is proposed, both boards immediately defer to their respective governments, and that is where the conversation dies.

The Indian government has not granted a No Objection Certificate for its cricket board to tour Pakistan since 2008. Pakistan last toured India for a bilateral series in 2012-13. Since then, the two nations have played each other only inside ICC-mandated tournaments or the Asia Cup, where political pressure to participate is harder to resist because pulling out means withdrawing from a multi-nation event rather than cancelling a bilateral arrangement.

The current geopolitical climate between the two countries is, to put it charitably, not warm. Cross-border tensions, diplomatic downgrades, and the broader security narrative all feed into government decision-making on both sides. Neither government, at this point in 2025, has shown any indication of softening its position enough to allow a bilateral series.

The PCB, for its part, has been vocal about wanting the series restored. Chairman Mohsin Naqvi has raised the issue in ICC forums and in media interactions. The BCCI, characteristically, has said very little publicly, which is itself a statement.

## What It Would Mean for Pakistan Cricket

The financial argument alone should accelerate every conversation. An India-Pakistan bilateral series, whether hosted in the UAE on a neutral basis or ideally on home soil for either team, would generate broadcast and sponsorship revenues on a scale that would transform the PCB's balance sheet. Pakistan cricket has chronically underperformed commercially relative to its on-field profile and its massive diaspora fanbase. An India series, even a short one, changes that equation dramatically.

There is also the competitive argument. Pakistan's red-ball and white-ball squads are in rebuilding phases. Babar Azam's international career has entered a period of scrutiny, and the team under Shan Masood in Tests is still searching for consistency. Playing India in a genuine bilateral context, with full series prestige on the line, would represent an accelerant for player development and public interest that no ICC event quite replicates. The pressure of a home bilateral series against India would test Pakistan's domestic structures in a way that tours of Zimbabwe or Ireland simply cannot.

## Scenarios Worth Watching

Three realistic scenarios exist, in ascending order of likelihood.

First, a full bilateral home-and-away series. This requires genuine diplomatic normalisation between the two governments. Given current trajectories, this is not a 2025 or 2026 prospect. File it under 'long-term optimism.'

Second, a neutral-venue bilateral series, most likely in the UAE. This has historical precedent, Pakistan played home series against India in Dubai and Abu Dhabi during the 2000s. A neutral venue sidesteps the security and political optics of hosting. This is more conceivable within a three-to-five year window if relations stabilise modestly.

Third, and most immediately likely, continued ICC-event-only contact. The 2025 Champions Trophy in Pakistan saw India play their matches from Dubai without travelling to Pakistan, which itself became a major controversy. That episode illustrated exactly how fraught even a minimal engagement can become. If the Champions Trophy model, with its separate-venue carve-out for India, is the best case available right now, then genuine bilateral cricket remains a distant aspiration.

The ICC's own leverage is limited. It can encourage, nudge, and structure event formats to maximise contact between the two sides. It cannot compel sovereign governments.

## The Bottom Line

Watch two things: the state of India-Pakistan diplomatic relations at the government level, and whether the BCCI's silence eventually gives way to any public signal of willingness. If both shift, even incrementally, the cricket boards will follow quickly enough. They know what the series is worth. The decision, as always, sits somewhere else entirely.
