The most commercially valuable bilateral series in world cricket has not been played since 2012-13. That is over a decade of frozen relations between two nuclear neighbours, and the vacuum has cost both boards, both sets of fans, and the sport itself something irreplaceable. The question is no longer whether Pakistan and India should play each other — everyone with a stake in cricket knows they should — but whether the conditions for it to actually happen can align within any foreseeable window.

## The Real Obstacle Is Not Cricket

When people say Pakistan and India cannot play bilateral cricket, they are really saying that the Indian government will not grant security clearance for a tour to Pakistan, and that the BCCI remains unwilling to push back against that position. Everything else, scheduling, broadcast rights, venue selection, is secondary noise.

The Board of Control for Cricket in India operates under a specific understanding: no bilateral series with Pakistan without clearance from the Government of India. That clearance has not come since the 2008 Mumbai attacks, and successive administrations in New Delhi have shown no appetite to revisit it. The PCB, to its credit, has consistently flagged this at ICC meetings, but flagging is not leverage. Pakistan's structural influence within the ICC has grown in recent years, partly through the Asia Cup standoff and partly through the Champions Trophy hosting rights awarded for 2025, but structural influence and political will are different currencies.

The Champions Trophy in February-March 2025 illustrated the ceiling of that influence perfectly. Pakistan hosted the event, India did not travel to Pakistan, and a hybrid model was cobbled together as a workaround. Both boards declared a version of victory. Neither side blinked on the core issue.

## What a Realistic Timeline Actually Looks Like

Three conditions would need to align for a bilateral series to happen, and none of them are close to being met simultaneously.

First, a sustained improvement in diplomatic relations. The two countries have seen brief warming periods before — the 2011 cricket diplomacy when Prime Minister Manmohan Singh watched the World Cup semi-final in Mohali, the 2015 Modi visit to Lahore — but warmth at the top has never translated into a cricket tour. For a series to materialise, the thaw would need to be deep enough to survive a general election cycle in India, which is a very high bar.

Second, a neutral venue arrangement both sides formally accept. The hybrid model is uncomfortable for the PCB because it legitimises India's refusal to tour. But if a permanent neutral venue, the UAE being the obvious candidate, were agreed upon as a standing arrangement rather than an emergency measure, it could provide a workaround that saves face on both sides. This is theoretically achievable, but the PCB would need to accept something it has publicly resisted.

Third, a commercial incentive large enough to embarrass governments into action. The IPL and PSL have both shown that cricket money moves fast when the opportunity is real. A bilateral Pakistan-India series, even in a neutral country, would generate broadcast revenues that dwarf almost any other cricket event. If a broadcaster or consortium were to bring a serious rights offer to the table, the economic pressure on both boards would intensify. That moment has not arrived yet, but it is the most likely forcing function.

A conservative estimate: if diplomatic relations do not materially improve before the 2027 ICC events cycle, a bilateral series before 2030 looks unlikely. If there is even a modest political opening, a neutral-venue series in the UAE could be arranged within 18 to 24 months of a government green light. The cricket is ready. The stadiums are ready. The audiences are certainly ready.

## What Pakistan Cricket Should Do in the Meantime

The PCB cannot afford to let the India question consume its strategic bandwidth. Babar Azam's Test side has a series of tours that matter enormously for Pakistan's ICC ranking and cycle points. The PSL continues to grow in credibility and needs further investment in infrastructure and talent pipelines. The Hyderabad Kingsmen and other franchises expanding the PSL's footprint into new cities represent a far more controllable and immediate opportunity for Pakistani cricket's development than chasing a bilateral series that depends on geopolitics.

That does not mean abandoning the push. The PCB should keep raising the issue formally at ICC meetings and should continue building the case, commercially and diplomatically, for a neutral-venue series as a stepping stone.

## What to Watch Next

Monitor the state of India-Pakistan diplomatic contact through 2025 and 2026. Watch whether the BCCI makes any quiet overtures ahead of the 2026 T20 World Cup, co-hosted by India and Sri Lanka, where both teams will share tournament space if they progress. That tournament will not be a bilateral series, but it may be the next genuine test of whether the political temperature has changed at all.
