Quetta Gladiators have built their PSL identity around one man more completely than any other franchise in the competition. Sarfaraz Ahmed is not simply their captain. He is their culture, their dressing room, their brand. That loyalty has delivered two PSL titles. It is also, increasingly, a conversation that Pakistan cricket needs to have honestly.

## The Real Story Is Not the Man, It Is the Model

Sarfaraz turned 37 in May. By the standards of wicketkeeper-batters, who carry one of the most physically punishing roles in the game, that is a significant number. His performances have not fallen off a cliff. He still reads the game sharply, he still motivates younger players, and his tactical instincts behind the stumps remain among the sharpest in domestic T20 cricket in Pakistan. But instinct and experience alone cannot paper over what any honest scout would notice: the explosive, match-turning innings that once defined his PSL contributions have become rarer.

Quetta's wider squad has aged alongside him. Several of the Pakistan internationals who anchored their title-winning campaigns are either past their peaks or have drifted out of the national picture. The overseas slots have carried a heavier burden as a result. When those picks misfire, as they inevitably do across a long franchise tournament, the local batting order has too often looked thin and predictable.

This is not a criticism of Sarfaraz as a person or as a leader. It is a structural question about how Quetta have managed their squad cycle, and whether the franchise has been bold enough to build a second generation around him rather than simply beside him.

## What His Captaincy Still Gives Them

Fairness demands this be said clearly: Sarfaraz's leadership inside the camp is genuinely difficult to replicate. Young Pakistani players, particularly those coming up through the Balochistan and southern Sindh pathways, respond to him. He has a directness that cuts through the nerves of franchise cricket, and his record of nurturing fast bowlers in particular is credible. There is a reason the PCB has kept returning to him even when the national selectors have looked elsewhere.

His reading of opposition batters and his ability to set attacking fields under pressure also remain high-level. In close games, Quetta under Sarfaraz rarely feel lost. The leadership deficit that some franchises expose in tense final overs is not Quetta's problem. Their problem is building enough match-winning firepower around that steady hand to make the captaincy matter in enough games.

## The Renewal Question

The Gladiators front office faces a choice that no franchise enjoys making when it involves a figure of Sarfaraz's stature. Either they begin a deliberate, planned transition toward a younger captain, embedding him in a mentoring role over the next one or two seasons, or they back him fully and reinvest in the overseas and emerging local talent around him so that his leadership has the tools to remain competitive.

Half-measures, the kind that see a legend retained out of respect but quietly sidelined in terms of squad resources, serve no one. They damage the player's legacy and they damage the franchise's results.

The PSL is a young man's competition in terms of tempo and physicality. The franchises winning titles consistently are those who cycle talent without sentiment. Lahore Qalandars' investment in their academy model is the clearest example of what deliberate planning produces. Quetta have not yet shown the same systematic approach to succession.

## What to Watch

The next PSL draft and retention window will be genuinely revealing. If Quetta commit serious resources to young Pakistani batting talent and give them protected roles alongside Sarfaraz, that signals the franchise understands the problem. If the squad sheet next season looks broadly similar to the last two, with overseas names carrying the batting load and the local core unchanged, it will confirm that sentiment is still driving decisions more than strategy.

Watch also whether Sarfaraz himself pushes for a transitional arrangement. He is smart enough to know his own timeline. A captain who manages his own succession is a rare thing in cricket, in Pakistan or anywhere. If he initiates that conversation, it would add one more chapter to what is already a remarkable career.

Quetta's loyalty to their captain is admirable. Now they need a plan that is equally loyal to the future of the franchise.
