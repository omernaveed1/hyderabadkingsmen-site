Multan Sultans have won more PSL titles than any franchise in the competition's history, and Mohammad Rizwan has been the heartbeat of every single one of those triumphs. But dominance in franchise cricket is a perishable commodity, and the question Pakistan cricket fans need to start asking honestly is whether the Sultans' peak is behind them or still ahead.

## The Angle: What This Era Actually Represents

Rizwan's Sultans are not a coincidence. They are a structure. Since the captaincy was handed to him, Multan have played with a coherent identity that most PSL franchises have never managed to build: disciplined batting from the top, a seam-heavy bowling attack suited to the conditions at Multan Cricket Stadium, and an unusually stable core squad that does not get dismantled every auction cycle.

That stability is the real story. Franchise cricket globally rewards teams that resist the temptation to panic-buy after a bad tournament. Multan, broadly speaking, have resisted it better than anyone else in the PSL. When you contrast their roster continuity with the revolving door of foreign signings that has plagued sides like Karachi Kings for the better part of a decade, the difference in philosophy becomes obvious.

Rizwan himself is the glue. His work ethic is almost absurdly well-documented at this point, but what gets less attention is his reading of bowling matchups and his ability to rotate strike against pace in the powerplay, which gives Multan's innings a shape that most T20 batting units lack. He is not just a captain. He is the team's tactical architecture made flesh.

## The Cracks Worth Watching

And yet. Dynasties do not announce their endings. They thin out quietly.

Rizwan is in his early thirties. His white-ball numbers remain elite, but the PSL is getting younger and faster. The overseas fast bowlers arriving in the competition are generating more pace, more skill, and more variation than the tournament has historically seen. Rizwan adapts well, but adaptation has limits, and even the very best batters in T20 cricket begin to see their strike rates squeezed once opposition analysts have three or four seasons of footage to work with.

The bowling attack is the bigger structural concern. Multan's seam battery has been effective, but it has also been heavily dependent on conditions. When they have played away from Multan, particularly in knockout stages on flat Karachi or Lahore pitches, the attack has looked noticeably thinner. That is a problem that title-winning teams eventually have to solve, and Multan have not fully solved it.

There is also the question of what happens if Rizwan's international commitments, or form, force him to miss matches. Multan's batting depth without him has occasionally looked precarious. Other PSL franchises have been building around younger Pakistani batters who could challenge Multan's supremacy within the next two or three seasons. Lahore Qalandars, for one, have a talent pipeline that should make Multan's management uncomfortable.

## What This Means Going Forward

For Multan, the next auction cycle and the next coaching appointment will be defining. If the franchise can bring in a power-hitting overseas bat who takes pressure off Rizwan at the top, and if they can identify a wrist-spinner or a left-arm quick who can be match-winning on flat decks, the dynasty has another chapter in it.

Rizwan himself has enough left. The real variable is whether the franchise's management matches his ambition. He is clearly not the sort of captain who accepts mediocrity quietly, and the PSL has seen more than one talented squad under-achieve because the administrative vision behind it was not equal to the playing talent in front of it.

## What to Watch Next

Three things will tell you very quickly where Multan's trajectory is headed. First, watch how they perform in knockout matches away from Multan. Second, watch whether their auction strategy prioritises depth or headline names. Third, and perhaps most tellingly, watch how Rizwan responds in the matches where Multan are under pressure early. Great captains reveal franchises. If he is still pulling games from difficult positions, the dynasty has more runway than the sceptics think.

Pakistan cricket has had very few genuine PSL dynasties. Multan are, so far, the closest thing to one. Whether that continues depends on decisions being made in boardrooms right now, not on the pitch. And in franchise cricket, that has always been where titles are really won and lost.
