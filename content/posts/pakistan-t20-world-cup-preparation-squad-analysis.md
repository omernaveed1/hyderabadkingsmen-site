Pakistan have never lacked talent in the shortest format. What they have consistently lacked is a squad that fires in the right moments at the right tournament. With the next T20 World Cup on the horizon, the question is not whether this group has the ability. It is whether selectors and team management can finally build a coherent, settled unit around that ability.

## The Real Story: Settling vs. Shuffling

Pakistan's T20 history is punctuated by the same recurring problem: selection instability. Players are picked, dropped, recalled, and repositioned in a cycle that makes it almost impossible to build genuine team chemistry. The current squad is no different. Babar Azam and Mohammad Rizwan remain the bedrock at the top of the order, their partnership producing runs with metronomic reliability. But the conversation has to move beyond those two. The national side cannot keep designing its entire batting blueprint around an opening stand.

The middle order is where Pakistan's T20 ambitions repeatedly come unstuck. Azam Khan has shown flashes of destructive power but has yet to prove he can anchor a chase or build an innings under sustained pressure. Usman Khan offers similar upside with similar uncertainty. Ifikhar Ahmed remains useful without ever being decisive in a crisis. Pakistan need at least two of these names to graduate from promising to dependable before any World Cup campaign can be taken seriously.

## Strengths Worth Believing In

The bowling attack, on its day, is among the most threatening in the world. Shaheen Shah Afridi, when fully fit and in rhythm, is a genuine match-winner with the new ball and at the death. Haris Rauf brings pace and aggression. Mohammad Amir's return to international cricket has added experience and left-arm variety. If all three are available and in form simultaneously, Pakistan's pace attack is as good as anything in the global T20 circuit.

Spin is the quieter strength. Shadab Khan, when his form holds, is a wicket-taking option who also contributes lower down the order. Abrar Ahmed's mystery spin adds an element of unpredictability that most international batting orders still find awkward.

Babar and Rizwan at the top, meanwhile, give Pakistan a platform most teams would envy. The issue is never the platform. It is what gets built on it.

## The Weaknesses Pakistan Cannot Afford to Ignore

Death bowling remains a chronic anxiety. Pakistan concede too many runs in the final four overs, particularly when Shaheen is off-colour or not operating at full throttle. No clear second death-bowling option has emerged to take that pressure off. Haris Rauf can be expensive at the death, and the lower end of the pace attack often looks makeshift in those moments when a tournament game is on the line.

Fielding is another area that rarely gets discussed with the seriousness it deserves. Dropped catches and fumbles in the deep have cost Pakistan dearly in knockout cricket. T20 World Cup matches are frequently decided by those margins, and no amount of batting brilliance recovers a game when three catches go down inside the powerplay.

The batting order also needs a finisher. Someone who can enter in the 15th over with 60 needed from 30 balls and get the job done without panicking. Pakistan have been searching for that profile for the better part of a decade. It remains unfilled.

## What Needs to Happen Before the Tournament

The priority has to be locking in the best eleven and playing them repeatedly. PSL performances will give selectors significant data on emerging talent, particularly around the middle-order and the death-bowling options. The Hyderabad Kingsmen, Lahore Qalandars, Karachi Kings and others will all provide evidence on players pushing for World Cup places. Domestic T20 competition has to be treated as a genuine selection filter rather than an afterthought.

Management also needs to decide what role Babar Azam plays beyond his batting. His captaincy record is mixed, and Pakistan cannot afford a situation where tactical uncertainty at the top bleeds into on-field paralysis.

## What to Watch Next

The upcoming bilateral T20 series before the World Cup are non-negotiable in terms of experimentation. That is where the middle-order permutations need to be tested under real pressure, not in dead rubbers late in a series. Watch how selectors respond if Azam Khan or Usman Khan underperform again. Watch whether a death-bowling solution actually emerges or whether Pakistan arrive at the World Cup hoping the problem does not expose itself.

Talent is not the issue. Organisation, nerve, and selection discipline are. Pakistan have the pieces. Whether they can assemble them coherently is the question that matters most.
