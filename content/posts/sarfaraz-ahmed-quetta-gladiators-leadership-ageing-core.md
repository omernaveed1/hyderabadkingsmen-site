Quetta Gladiators have always done things their own way. Two PSL titles, a culture of continuity, and Sarfaraz Ahmed at the centre of almost everything. That model produced glory. Whether it still can is the question Pakistan cricket needs to ask honestly.

## The Steady Hand That Built a Dynasty

Sarfaraz Ahmed is, by any fair measure, one of the greatest PSL captains the competition has produced. He led Quetta to back-to-back titles in 2016 and 2017, marshalling resources intelligently and extracting performances from players others had written off. His reading of the game behind the stumps remains sharp, his presence in the dressing room authoritative. In a franchise environment that often chews through captains on short cycles, his longevity at Quetta is remarkable.

But longevity and relevance are not always the same thing.

## What This Story Is Really About

The real issue is not Sarfaraz's ability as an individual. It is whether Quetta, as a franchise, has quietly become too comfortable around a core that is ageing together.

Sarfaraz is in his mid-thirties. Several of the players who have formed Quetta's backbone across multiple seasons are in the same bracket. That kind of experienced nucleus can be enormously valuable in a knockout game when nerves are fraying, but across the grind of a PSL group stage, it can also mean a side that is slightly slower in the field, slightly more predictable in its batting approach, and slightly less willing to gamble on the high-risk, high-reward cricket that the format increasingly demands.

T20 cricket is evolving at a pace that punishes franchises who stand still. The template for winning it has shifted noticeably in recent seasons. Power-hitting depth, athletic fielding, and the ability to absorb pressure through aggression rather than accumulation are now baseline expectations, not differentiators. Quetta's identity, built on grit, experience, and tactical nous, is admirable. It may no longer be sufficient on its own.

There is also a succession question that hovers quietly over the Gladiators. Who leads Quetta after Sarfaraz? The franchise has not obviously developed an answer. In the absence of a clear succession plan, a side risks being caught mid-transition, which is precisely the worst moment to lose a long-serving captain.

## What It Means Going Forward

For Sarfaraz personally, the challenge is to reinvent his role slightly without being asked to surrender it entirely. The best franchise captains in this phase of their careers learn to lead with a slightly longer rope, giving younger batting talent room to play without the constant weight of the captain's expectations. Sarfaraz has always been intense and hands-on. That intensity is an asset. Calibrating it to bring the next generation along rather than carry them is where the real leadership challenge now lies.

For Quetta as a franchise, the calculus is harder. Rebuilding around a new captain mid-cycle risks disrupting whatever chemistry still exists. Not rebuilding risks gradual decline. The honest answer is probably a hybrid approach, bringing in two or three high-impact younger players specifically around Sarfaraz rather than replacing him, so that when the transition does come, the architecture is already in place.

The Gladiators' recruitment strategy in recent seasons will tell the real story. If they continue to prioritise proven names over exciting emerging ones, the trajectory is already set.

## What to Watch Next

Watch Quetta's squad announcements ahead of the next PSL season carefully. The balance between experience and youth in their overseas slots will be revealing. If the franchise begins investing in younger Pakistani talent in the middle order and doubling down on fielding quality, it signals that someone inside the organisation has diagnosed the problem. If the squad looks broadly familiar, the status quo remains.

Also watch Sarfaraz's own form with the bat. His lower-order contributions can still shift close games, and a Sarfaraz in form is a different proposition as a captain, more confident, more willing to back instinct in the field. His batting form and Quetta's fortunes have historically moved together more than the numbers always show.

The Gladiators are not a franchise in crisis. They are a franchise at a crossroads, which is actually a more interesting and more dangerous place to be. Sarfaraz Ahmed has navigated bigger moments than this. Whether the organisation around him has the appetite for honest self-assessment is the real question.
