Pakistan cricket has produced fast bowlers the way Punjab produces wheat, abundantly and with occasional brilliance. But Naseem Shah is something rarer: a genuine red-ball spearhead with the pace, seam, and temperament to win Test matches on his own. Which is precisely why the PCB's handling of his workload has become the most consequential selection debate in Pakistan cricket right now.

## The Real Issue

This is not simply a fitness question. Naseem has dealt with shoulder injuries that would have ended lesser careers at his age, and he has come back swinging each time. The deeper issue is structural. Pakistan's Test schedule is punishing, their fast-bowling depth behind Naseem is thin, and the temptation to use him in white-ball formats, where he is equally lethal, pulls in the opposite direction of preservation.

The PCB faces a choice that no amount of committee meetings can paper over. Do you manage Naseem conservatively in red-ball cricket and accept a weakened Test attack in the short term, or do you run him hard while he is at peak fitness and risk the kind of breakdown that costs you eighteen months of a 24-year-old's career?

History argues strongly for caution. Waqar Younis and Shoaib Akhtar were both brilliant and both broken by overuse, not through carelessness exactly, but through the absence of a coherent workload framework. Pakistan cannot afford to repeat that cycle with the best fast-bowling prospect it has produced in a generation.

## The Workload Numbers Tell a Story

Naseem has bowled in Tests, ODIs, and T20Is within the same bilateral series windows on multiple occasions. For a fast bowler recovering from a serious shoulder operation, that kind of cross-format congestion is not ideal. High-intensity short-form bowling, especially the death-overs demands of T20 cricket, puts different stresses on the shoulder and elbow than long red-ball spells, but it is cumulative fatigue that eventually bites.

The PSL compounds the problem. Franchise cricket pays well and the PCB has contractual and commercial interests in seeing its marquee players visible throughout the tournament. Naseem's involvement with his PSL franchise is not the villain here, but it is one more variable the selectors and the team physio need to factor in when planning a Test tour that might follow within weeks.

The PCB's high-performance and medical staff are not without resources. The system exists. The question is whether the selectors and team management have the discipline to follow the data when the pressure of a big series is bearing down on them.

## What It Means Going Forward

If Pakistan wants Naseem operating at full tilt in Test cricket for the next eight to ten years, the blueprint is fairly straightforward even if it is politically uncomfortable. Rotate him out of at least some T20I assignments during heavy Test tour periods. Set hard over-count limits in domestic and franchise cricket that feed into his Test preparation windows. And resist the urge to play him in dead-rubber ODIs when a series is already decided.

The longer-term upside is substantial. A fit, fresh Naseem Shah leading a Test attack through a full tour of England or Australia, where overhead conditions and extra bounce reward everything he does well, is one of the more exciting prospects in world cricket. A Naseem strapped with miles on his body by February and asked to spearhead a Boxing Day Test is a different calculation entirely.

The selectors also need a contingency. Khurram Shahzad and Mohammad Ali have shown they can contribute at Test level, but neither is a like-for-like replacement if Naseem breaks down mid-series. Building those alternatives into regular Test squads, even when Naseem is available, is not an admission of doubt. It is basic planning.

## What to Watch Next

The immediate indicator will be how the PCB deploys Naseem in the next multi-format series Pakistan plays. If he turns up in all three formats back to back without a structured break, it signals that workload management remains aspirational rather than operational. If the selectors rest him from T20Is while preserving him for Tests, that is a meaningful sign the lessons have landed.

Watch also for his over counts per innings in the red-ball game. A spearhead regularly bowling 25-plus overs in a Test innings is a fast bowler being asked to carry too much. Pakistan's batting and spin bowling need to share that burden.

Naseem Shah is not just a selection pick. He is a long-term national asset. The PCB's job is to treat him like one.
