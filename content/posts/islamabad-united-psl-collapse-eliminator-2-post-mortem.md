Islamabad United entered the business end of this PSL season as the team everyone feared. They left it as a cautionary tale about peaking too early, with an Eliminator 2 defeat ending a campaign that had promised so much more.

## The Real Story: Momentum Is a Mirage

Winning the league stage in PSL is worth real points on the table, but history is littered with teams that found the knockout rounds an entirely different sport. Islamabad United learned that lesson the hard way.

Their league-stage dominance was built on a combination of disciplined bowling rotations, a settled top order, and the kind of confidence that comes from winning close games. Those attributes did not disappear overnight. What changed was the margin for error. In knockout cricket, one bad powerplay or one dropped catch does not get corrected by five more group fixtures. It ends your season on the spot.

That is precisely what happened. When United needed their decision-making to be sharpest, it was shakiest. Batting collapses that were papered over during the league stage became match-defining in the eliminators. Bowling plans that worked against familiar opponents were read and countered by sides who had scouted United meticulously across nine or ten league encounters.

## The Batting Fragility That Was Always There

United's top order is genuinely dangerous. The problem, as sharper observers noted even during the league phase, was that their middle order never convincingly answered the question of what happens when the powerplay goes wrong.

Several times across the tournament, a top-three dismissal precipitated a wobble that only a cameo from lower down salvaged. In the league stage, those wobbles were survivable. In knockout cricket, the opposition's best death bowlers are available for full quotas and captains set attacking fields precisely because they have nothing to lose. United's middle-order batters, some of whom carried modest PSL records coming in, were exposed in that environment.

The franchise will need an honest audit of whether the balance of their squad, heavy on express pace and quality at the top of the batting, is built for a full campaign or for the comfort of favourable conditions.

## The Bowling Rotation Question

United's bowling was their stronger suit, and yet the eliminators exposed a dependency on conditions. When pitches offered carry and pace, their quicks were imposing. On the slower surfaces that tend to appear once the tournament reaches its Lahore or Karachi finals venues, their lengths looked slightly askew and their variations less reliable.

Spin, in particular, became a vulnerability rather than a weapon. Opponents targeted their spinners with reverse sweeps and slog-sweeps from the second over of any spin spell. In the earlier rounds, United had the luxury of reverting to pace at those moments. In a knockout, bowling changes become desperate rather than strategic, and opponents sense it.

## What This Means for the Franchise Going Forward

Islamabad United remain one of the two or three best-run franchises in the PSL. Their recruitment model, their coaching structure, and their ability to blend local talent with overseas quality have made them consistent contenders. None of that changes because of one disappointing eliminator.

But this exit should sharpen some questions the franchise has perhaps been avoiding. The core of this squad is not young. Several key contributors are on the back half of their T20 careers. If United want another title, the rebuild needs to begin now, not reactively after next year's draft.

The domestic pipeline also matters here. Pakistan cricket needs United to develop two or three nationally relevant players each season. This campaign did not obviously deliver that. A young fast bowler or an emerging middle-order batter given real responsibility in a United shirt is worth more to the ecosystem than a veteran import filling a gap comfortably.

## What to Watch Next

The immediate focus shifts to how United's key players translate their PSL form, or lack of it, into national selection calculations. Selectors for Pakistan's upcoming schedule will be watching closely. A player who collapses under knockout pressure for his franchise raises a legitimate question about whether he is ready for Test or ODI cricket.

For the franchise itself, the Platinum and Diamond category decisions at next year's draft will define the next cycle. Whether they retain their core or go younger will tell observers everything about how seriously they absorbed the lessons of this early exit.

Leading the league stage only to fall in Eliminator 2 is not just a bad result. It is a signal that something structural needs addressing. United have the resources and the intelligence to address it. The question is whether they will.
