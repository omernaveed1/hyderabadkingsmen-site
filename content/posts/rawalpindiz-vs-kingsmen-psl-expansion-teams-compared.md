Two new franchises. One league. Wildly different stories so far.

The PSL's expansion to eight teams was always going to be its boldest structural experiment. Rawalpindiz and the Hyderabad Kingsmen arrived together as the league's newest members, carrying the weight of expectation from two passionate cricket cities. Weeks into their debut seasons, the two clubs offer a fascinating study in contrasts — and a sharp lesson in what it actually takes to build a franchise from scratch in Pakistan's premier T20 competition.

## The Same Starting Line, Different Trajectories

Both franchises entered the PSL draft with identical constraints: no inherited squad, no established fan culture, no institutional memory. Everything had to be built from zero. That is harder than it sounds in a league where the six existing teams have had years to identify local talent pipelines, forge coaching philosophies, and build winning dressing-room cultures.

What separated the two clubs from day one was squad construction philosophy. The Kingsmen leaned into their regional identity, prioritising players with Sindh and Hyderabad domestic backgrounds alongside their overseas picks. Rawalpindiz, anchored in the Pindi heartland, faced a different challenge: Rawalpindi is a city that has historically exported talent to other franchises rather than fielding its own. Names that Pindi fans might have expected to see in Rawalpindiz colours were already contracted elsewhere, forcing the new franchise to be creative — or opportunistic, depending on how generously you view their draft strategy.

## Batting on Resources, Bowling on Hope

At the batting end, Rawalpindiz have shown glimpses of genuine firepower. Their top order has had moments that suggest the franchise identified the right profile of aggressive, big-ground hitters suited to the fast Pindi surface. But consistency has been the problem. A T20 franchise lives and dies by how often its batting unit fires as a collective, and the Rawalpindiz top order has looked brilliant one evening and brittle the next.

The Kingsmen have had comparable teething troubles. Building a new franchise means there is no senior leadership figure who has been through multiple PSL seasons with the club, no unwritten code about how the team responds to a collapse. Both sides have found that out the hard way.

On bowling, neither team has yet demonstrated the variety that the established franchises take for granted. Depth in the pace department, a reliable mystery spinner, a death-bowling specialist who the captain trusts unconditionally — those assets take seasons to assemble. Rawalpindiz and the Kingsmen are both working from a thinner inventory than their rivals, and it shows in the middle overs and at the death.

## The Coaching Equation

One area where a proper assessment requires more transparency from both franchises is the coaching and support structure. In franchise T20 cricket globally, the head coach's influence on squad culture in year one is enormous. A new franchise with an inexperienced coach is a very different proposition from a new franchise with a battle-hardened T20 operator in the dugout. Neither Rawalpindiz nor the Kingsmen have made their backroom operations a story, which is probably deliberate — but it is also something supporters and analysts will need to watch as both clubs evolve.

## What Success Actually Looks Like in Year One

Here is the honest truth that both sets of supporters need to hear: for a debut PSL franchise, finishing dead last is not a catastrophe. Lahore Qalandars were routinely wooden-spooners before they became champions. Islamabad United's early dominance was the outlier, not the template.

Success for Rawalpindiz and the Kingsmen in their first season is measured differently. How many young domestic players got meaningful PSL minutes? Did the franchise identify its future captain? Did the squad show competitive enough cricket to attract better overseas names next year? By those standards, neither side has embarrassed itself, and neither has fully delivered yet.

The Rawalpindiz home advantage at Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium — assuming they get meaningful home fixtures — is a genuine asset. The Kingsmen's Hyderabad base, still developing its PSL hosting credentials, is a longer-term project.

## What to Watch Next

The second half of the season will reveal which franchise has the sharper instinct for in-tournament adaptation. Rawalpindiz will need their batting to find consistency before they can realistically challenge for the play-offs. The Kingsmen face the same deadline. Both franchises should be using every remaining fixture as a scouting mission for the next draft as much as a push for points.

The real verdict on PSL's two newest members will not arrive this season. It will come when one of them lifts the trophy. Patience is not just a virtue in franchise cricket. It is the entry requirement.
