Australia have added pace options Henry Thornton and Riley Meredith to their T20I squad for the five-match series against England, with Thornton in line for a debut. For Pakistan, tracking how rival nations build T20 pace depth is not mere curiosity — it is preparation.

## The Real Story: Pace Stocks and the Global T20 Arms Race

This is not simply an Australian selection story. It is a reminder of how the top nations are constantly refreshing their short-format pace resources, rotating through their domestic pipelines and stress-testing new names before major ICC events. Australia, even after the 2024 T20 World Cup cycle, are not standing still.

Henry Thornton has earned his opportunity through consistent performances in the Big Bash League. He is a tall, skiddy right-arm quick who generates awkward bounce and can hit the pitch hard at the death. Riley Meredith, on the other hand, is not new to international cricket — he is a re-entry, a bowler of genuine pace who has had fitness and consistency questions hanging over him for years. His inclusion signals that Australia's selectors remain willing to back raw speed when it is available.

The timing matters. Australia are playing England in a T20I series that serves as preparation ahead of a busy international calendar. They are experimenting now so they do not have to experiment later.

## Why Pakistan Cricket Should Pay Attention

Pakistan's own T20I pace attack is in a state of flux. Shaheen Shah Afridi remains the spearhead, but the supporting cast, particularly at the death, has been inconsistent. Naseem Shah's fitness has been a persistent concern. Haris Rauf continues to be serviceable but rarely devastating in the powerplay. Abbas Afridi and Mohammad Hasnain have flashed potential without fully nailing down roles.

Australia's approach of cycling in bowlers like Thornton — who bring something different in terms of trajectory and angles — is worth studying. Pakistan's domestic circuit, the PSL included, produces pace talents regularly, but the bridge between franchise cricket and consistent international selection has often been shaky.

The PSL remains the most visible audition stage for Pakistan's next generation of quicks. Franchises like the Hyderabad Kingsmen, Lahore Qalandars and Peshawar Zalmi have historically surfaced raw pace talent. But surfacing them is only step one. Australia's model suggests that the next step, giving them live international exposure early and often, is where Pakistan's selectors could be more aggressive.

## What It Means Going Forward

If Thornton makes his debut in this series and performs, it adds another quality seamer to an already deep Australian T20I pool. That depth is a strategic asset. When they arrive in Pakistan for future bilateral series, or when both sides meet in ICC knockouts, Pakistan's batters will face bowlers they may have limited data on.

For Pakistan's T20I side, the lesson is structural. The national selectors under the current setup need to identify one or two untested pace options right now and begin integrating them into the T20I fold. Waiting for a perfect moment before blooding a new quick is a luxury no side can afford in the current format.

The Pakistan men's T20I schedule post-Champions Trophy 2025 is packed. There is no better window than bilateral series to do what Australia are doing here — broadening the pace base, creating competition for places and ensuring no single bowler becomes irreplaceable through lack of alternatives.

## What to Watch Next

First, watch how Thornton performs if handed his debut. His BBL numbers suggest he can be a genuine wicket-taking option in the powerplay and at the death, the two phases where Pakistan have most often leaked runs.

Second, watch whether the PCB's selection committee responds in kind when Pakistan's next T20I assignments come around. The temptation will be to recall proven names. The smarter move is to give a young quick, someone from the most recent PSL season or the domestic circuit, a live audition.

Third, watch the broader Australia versus England series for tactical cues. How opposing captains set fields against pace-heavy attacks, how batters counter steep-angle deliveries, these are live laboratories for Pakistan's coaching staff to draw from.

Australia are sharpening their tools. Pakistan cannot afford to let theirs go blunt.
