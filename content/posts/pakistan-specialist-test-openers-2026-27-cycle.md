Pakistan's Test batting has carried a structural fault for the better part of a decade, and it sits right at the top of the order. The 2026-27 international cycle, which is expected to include tours of significant difficulty, makes it an urgent priority for the selectors to identify and back genuine, specialist Test openers rather than keep recycling middle-order batters who are asked to absorb the new ball as a favour.

## The Real Problem Is Philosophical

This is not simply a talent shortage. Pakistan has produced technically correct batters in domestic red-ball cricket who have opened consistently for their provincial sides. The deeper issue is a selection philosophy that has repeatedly reached for established names, comfortable faces, or white-ball reputations when the top-order spot comes up. The result is a revolving door: Imam-ul-Haq, Abdullah Shafique, Zaman Khan shuffled around the batting card, Fakhar Zaman inserted as an experiment, Saim Ayub handed the role almost by default because he was in form at the time.

None of that is a coherent plan. A specialist Test opener is not someone who bats at the top of the order when nothing better is available. He is someone who trains specifically for the discipline, who studies new-ball bowlers, who understands conditions overseas, and who is judged over a meaningful run of matches rather than dropped after two bad series.

## What the 2026-27 Cycle Demands

Without specifying every fixture on the horizon, it is fair to say that Pakistan's upcoming Test commitments are weighted toward subcontinent conditions and at least one significant away challenge. Both contexts punish an unsettled opening pair in different ways. At home on turning surfaces, an opener who cannot read spin from the second session onward becomes a liability before the top of the batting order is even properly set. Away, against quality pace in conditions that assist the new ball, you need openers who have the technique and the temperament to bat time.

Shaheen Shah Afridi's bowling, Naseem Shah's pace, Hasan Ali's variations — the bowling attack is not the crisis. The crisis is that too often by the time the lower middle order arrives, Pakistan are three or four down inside the first hour. A settled, specialist opening partnership would change the entire character of Pakistan's Test innings.

## Who Fits the Brief

The domestic circuit, particularly the Quaid-e-Azam Trophy, has thrown up openers who deserve longer looks at the Test level. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Central Punjab in particular have had red-ball openers with high first-class averages who have not been given more than a handful of caps before selectors lost patience. The PSL is not the right lens for picking Test openers, and yet it remains an informal influence on selection thinking. That is a category error. A batter who thrives in a powerplay against a T20 field is solving a completely different problem from an opener who must negotiate a Dukes ball with two slips and a gully in place.

Saim Ayub has real potential and his technique deserves respect. But even he has sometimes been treated as an experiment rather than a project. The difference matters. An experiment ends when results disappoint. A project is protected, coached, and allowed to mature.

## A Simple Ask: Consistency and Courage

The selectors need to do two things. First, identify two specialist Test openers from the domestic red-ball pool, using Quaid-e-Azam Trophy performance as the primary filter. Second, back them through a minimum of two full series before reassessing. The courage to stay with a selection when the first Test goes badly is exactly the kind of institutional nerve Pakistan's batting environment has lacked.

Shaheen, Naseem and the rest can only do so much. At some point, Pakistan's batting has to hold a match situation, and that starts at the top of the order.

## What to Watch

The next Quaid-e-Azam Trophy cycle is the clearest signal to watch. If the selectors send observers to provincial red-ball cricket with a genuine brief to find top-order specialists, that changes the conversation. If the next Test squad is again shaped primarily by white-ball form and familiar names, then the opening problem will still be unsolved when Pakistan's toughest fixtures of 2026-27 arrive.

The time to fix the roof is before the rain.
