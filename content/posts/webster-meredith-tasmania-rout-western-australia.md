Beau Webster and Riley Meredith produced a ruthless combined bowling performance to dismantle Western Australia for a meagre 115, with Tasmania completing a commanding one-wicket chase inside 17 overs. The result is another reminder of the depth Australia's domestic circuit is quietly producing, and for Pakistan cricket observers, it carries relevance beyond Sheffield Shield standings.

## The Real Story: Webster's All-Round Case Gets Louder

For months now, Beau Webster has been doing the kind of things that make national selectors sit up. His role in this demolition was not a one-off cameo — it was a continuation of a pattern. When a player is routinely hurting opposition batting lineups and then contributing meaningfully with the bat, the conversation around him inevitably expands from domestic standout to genuine international candidate.

Why does this matter to Pakistani cricket followers? Because Webster has already been linked to Australia's Test plans, and any player pushing for a spot in the Australian XI is worth tracking closely. Pakistan and Australia remain locked in a fierce bilateral rivalry, and understanding the shape of Australia's squad depth is part of reading the broader game. A reinvigorated Australia, with allrounders capable of winning matches across both disciplines, is a more complicated opponent. Pakistan's selectors and coaching staff would be foolish not to take note.

Riley Meredith, meanwhile, is a different kind of story. Raw pace has always been his calling card, and in Australian domestic conditions, that quality alone can make batters look ordinary. Skittling a side for 115 is not simply a matter of bowling fast — it requires discipline, the right lines, and the ability to build pressure in partnerships. Meredith showed all of that here. His trajectory from PSL pitches — where he has featured and impressed with his express pace — to being a consistent wicket-taker in Australian first-class cricket is worth following for PSL franchise scouts and Pakistan's own fast-bowling coaches looking at opposition threats.

## Collapsing for 115: What It Tells Us About WA

Western Australia's 115 all out is a collective batting failure, not just a story of two bowlers in form. A total that low in a domestic fixture suggests either extreme conditions, a batting unit that panicked, or both. Tasmania's bowlers exploited it ruthlessly, but the innings also raises questions about WA's middle-order resilience — the sort of vulnerability that good bowling attacks, including those in the PSL, train specifically to expose.

For cricket analysts in Pakistan watching this closely, the collapse has echoes of similar failures we have seen in T20 franchise cricket. When batting lineups face sustained short-pitched aggression from a bowler of Meredith's pace, they either have a plan or they do not. WA's batters, on this occasion, clearly did not.

## What Happens Next

For Webster, the next logical step is sustained selection pressure on the Australian national setup. If he continues performing at this level, the question will stop being whether he deserves a look and start being which format suits him best. His allround value — genuine with both bat and ball — gives selectors options that a specialist rarely offers.

For Meredith, the goal is fitness and consistency. He has always had the ability; keeping himself on the park and replicating this kind of form across a full season would make him impossible to ignore. PSL franchises who have used him previously will be watching.

For Tasmania, this result strengthens their position and their collective confidence. A team that bowls out opponents for 115 and then chases down the target one down, in 16.2 overs, is a unit playing with clarity and purpose.

## What to Watch

Keep an eye on Webster's next few outings. If he maintains this form heading into Australia's upcoming international schedule, the case for his inclusion becomes a genuine talking point rather than a hypothetical. For PSL observers, Meredith's availability windows and which franchise secures him in future drafts will be worth monitoring. Fast bowlers who can do this in Sheffield Shield cricket tend to carry that authority into franchise T20 cricket as well.

Australia's domestic pipeline is clearly producing. Pakistan's own domestic structure, and whether it can produce similar match-winning allrounders who dominate at first-class level before graduating to international cricket, remains the more urgent and uncomfortable question.
