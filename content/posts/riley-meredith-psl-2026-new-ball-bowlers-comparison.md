Riley Meredith is fast. That much is not in dispute. But fast alone does not win you a PSL powerplay, and with eight franchises set to unleash some serious new-ball firepower in PSL 2026, the Australian right-armer will need more than raw pace to stand above the competition.

## The Real Question Here

Every PSL season produces a headline overseas quick — the one name fans talk about before a ball is bowled. Meredith has the profile for that role. He consistently touches 145-150 km/h, he has an awkward, scrambled-seam approach that troubles top-order batters, and he has been around franchise cricket long enough to know what a Lahore pitch at dusk asks of you.

But PSL 2026 is not short of pace. The new-ball landscape across the competition is arguably deeper than it has been in years, and Meredith's edge is not as wide as his reputation might suggest.

## The Comparison

**Meredith vs Shaheen Shah Afridi.** Shaheen remains the benchmark. Whatever franchise secures him gets a bowler who swings it at pace, takes wickets in the first two overs with embarrassing regularity, and brings genuine leadership to an attack. He is a left-armer who creates angles Meredith cannot replicate. On pitches that offer any assistance, Shaheen is the single best new-ball option in the PSL pool. Meredith's advantage is raw speed; Shaheen's is craft plus speed. That is not a close contest.

**Meredith vs Naseem Shah.** Naseem is the more interesting comparison. Both are aggressive, both look to hit the hard length, and both can bowl a bouncer that genuinely threatens a batter's helmet rather than their ego. Naseem, however, is a Pakistani domestic product with deep familiarity of local conditions, and he moves the ball off the seam more consistently than Meredith. If Naseem is fully fit and in form, he edges Meredith on most PSL surfaces. The caveat: Naseem's fitness has been a recurring concern. That uncertainty opens the door.

**Meredith vs Mohammad Amir.** Amir's return to franchise cricket has been watched with interest. He is no longer the bowler who dismantled New Zealand in 2016, but his first-over threat remains real. He swings both ways, reads the pitch quickly, and understands the powerplay game deeply. Where Meredith wins is the second spell. Amir's pace is in the 130s now; Meredith at full throttle in the middle overs is a different proposition entirely.

**Meredith vs Haris Rauf.** Rauf might actually be Meredith's closest rival for the title of most dangerous overseas-eligible or draft-available new-ball quick in PSL 2026. Rauf is consistently quick, has a hard length that plays in Pakistan, and has matured tactically over his PSL seasons. The edge Meredith holds is shape: Rauf tends to be straighter; Meredith's awkward release angle creates different problems for right-handers in particular.

**Meredith vs the overseas pool (Trent Boult, Maheesh Theekshana et al.).** Boult is a powerplay artist if he features, but left-arm swing of his variety is a different skill set entirely. Theekshana is a spinner and irrelevant to this comparison. Among pace options from outside Pakistan, Meredith is probably near the top of the available pile, which is exactly why he attracts franchise interest.

## What This Means Going Forward

For whichever franchise deploys Meredith in 2026, the template should be clear: use him in the powerplay on hard, good-length pitches, and bring him back for a two-over spell at the death. He is not a full-quota bowler in the way Shaheen or Naseem are; he is a weapon to be pointed at specific moments. Franchise coaches who try to use him as a workhorse risk blunting the very thing that makes him valuable.

His biggest challenge will be conditions. PSL pitches, particularly later in the tournament, tend to get slower and lower. Meredith's pace-into-the-pitch game requires something off the surface. On flat, dry tracks he can become expensive quickly.

## What to Watch

Watch Meredith's economy rate in overs one and two versus his economy in overs three and four of the powerplay. That split will tell you whether he is genuinely troubling top-order batters or simply bowling fast at them. Watch also how franchises with Shaheen or Naseem available use their overseas quick slot: if they spend it on Meredith, that says something real about the depth of the domestic Pakistani fast-bowling pool right now.

Riley Meredith is a top-tier PSL new-ball option. He is not the top-tier option. In a competition with Shaheen, Naseem, and Rauf available, that distinction matters.
