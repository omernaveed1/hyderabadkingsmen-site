Sharjeel Khan has never lacked for runs when given the opportunity. What he has always lacked is a sustained window to prove himself, and in PSL 2026 he appears determined to make that problem impossible to ignore.

## The Runs Are Back

Sharjeel is attacking from the first ball again — the wide arc through mid-wicket, the flat-batted swipe over extra cover, the almost contemptuous pull off anything short. It is the same player who made Pakistan sit up in 2016 and early 2017 before a spot-fixing ban turned what looked like a long international career into a cautionary tale. He served his time, returned to domestic cricket, and has since existed in a strange purgatory: too good to ignore completely, never quite impossible to overlook.

PSL 2026 is changing that calculus. Sharjeel is finding the boundary consistently at the top of the order, giving his franchise genuine firepower in the powerplay — the phase that separates contenders from also-rans in T20 cricket. At 35, he is not the future. But in a format decided in six-over bursts, he may be exactly the present Pakistan needs.

## What This Story Is Really About

This is not simply a feel-good redemption arc. Pakistan's T20I batting has a structural problem that keeps reasserting itself at the top of the order. The selectors want Babar Azam and Mohammad Rizwan to anchor and accelerate, but international bowling attacks have learned to dry up the middle overs against that template. Pakistan need someone who arrives at the crease prepared to take the game away in the first six overs regardless of conditions or reputation.

Sharjeel does exactly that. He is not a nuanced, situation-reading opener. He is a designated destroyer, and that is a specific, scarce commodity. The problem is that Pakistan's selection culture has long rewarded patience and accumulation over destruction, and a player with Sharjeel's past faces additional scrutiny that others simply do not.

But numbers do not carry asterisks. If he finishes PSL 2026 as one of the tournament's leading run-scorers and strike-rate leaders in the powerplay, the selectors will have to articulate publicly why he is not in the frame, and that conversation will be uncomfortable.

## What It Means Going Forward

For Sharjeel personally, this is almost certainly a last realistic shot at a Pakistan recall. The window for a 35-year-old T20 opener, however destructive, does not stay open indefinitely. He needs the runs to keep coming in knockout matches, not just in group-stage encounters against weaker attacks. Runs against quality pace in pressure games are the currency that buys a selector's conviction.

For Pakistan T20I cricket more broadly, his resurgence raises a structural question the team management needs to answer honestly. The squad has rotated through several opening combinations since the 2022 T20 World Cup semi-final exit. None has fully solved the powerplay problem in the absence of a batter willing to play on the front foot of risk from ball one. Sharjeel is that batter. If he is not selected, the selectors owe the public a coherent explanation of who they believe fills that role instead.

There is also the matter of the franchise he is representing. Strong powerplay batting lifts a team's entire innings structure, and the ripple effect of Sharjeel's form this PSL will be felt well beyond his own scorecard.

## What to Watch Next

Three things will define whether this PSL run translates into a genuine national conversation.

First, watch how Sharjeel performs when tournament pressure increases. Eliminator and final conditions separate form from character, and he needs to deliver there.

Second, watch the ICC event calendar. Pakistan's next major T20I assignment will shape how urgently selectors feel the need to experiment. A short bilateral series is one thing; an ICC tournament is another entirely.

Third, watch Babar Azam's own PSL 2026 form. If Babar fires at the top of the order and the broader Pakistan batting blueprint looks settled, the case for disruption weakens. If Babar struggles, the appetite for alternatives grows sharply.

Sharjeel Khan has earned moments of optimism before and seen them evaporate. PSL 2026 looks like his most convincing chapter since the comeback. Whether Pakistan's selectors are watching with genuine open minds, or merely with polite indifference, is the real question worth asking.
