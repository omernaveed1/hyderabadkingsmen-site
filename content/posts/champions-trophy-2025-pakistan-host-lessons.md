Pakistan's hosting of the 2025 Champions Trophy was always going to be freighted with history. Twenty-nine years had passed since a global ICC event was staged on Pakistani soil, and every match carried the weight of a nation desperate to prove it belongs among the sport's premier hosts. The verdict, now that the dust has settled, is complicated — genuinely impressive in places, avoidable in others.

## The Real Story: Beyond the Politics

Much of the pre-tournament conversation was consumed by the hybrid model forced on the PCB after India refused to travel to Pakistan. That arrangement, whatever one thinks of its fairness, defined the optics before a single ball was bowled. But strip away the geopolitics and a clearer picture emerges: Pakistan staged competitive, well-attended cricket that reminded the world why this country produces such passionate fans. The question is whether the organisational machinery matched that passion.

## What Pakistan Got Right

**The crowds.** Lahore and Karachi delivered full or near-full houses for the marquee matches hosted on Pakistani soil. The Gaddafi Stadium in particular was electric in a way that neutral-venue cricket simply cannot replicate. Capacity crowds, colour, and noise — Pakistan's fans have never been the problem.

**Stadium infrastructure.** Both Lahore and Rawalpindi had been upgraded significantly ahead of the tournament. Floodlights, playing surfaces, and broadcast facilities drew broadly positive reviews from television rights holders. The pitches offered varied, competitive conditions rather than flat featherbeds, which enhanced the quality of cricket.

**Security execution.** International teams arrived and departed safely. The logistical operation around team movements, training arrangements, and venue security was managed without serious incident. For a country still battling an unfair security perception abroad, this matters enormously and should not be understated.

**Volunteer and ground staff culture.** Reports from visiting journalists pointed to warmth and professionalism from local ground staff and volunteers, the kind of human texture that makes an event feel genuinely welcoming.

## What Pakistan Got Wrong

**The hybrid model acceptance.** This is a structural and diplomatic failure that predates the tournament but shaped it entirely. The PCB ultimately accepted an arrangement that saw India's matches moved to Dubai, diluting the event's identity as a Pakistan-hosted tournament. The board's negotiating position, whatever constraints existed, produced an outcome that undermined the very purpose of hosting rights. Future events require a clearer line drawn well in advance.

**Ticketing chaos.** Reports of ticket distribution problems, inconsistent pricing, and secondary-market exploitation surfaced repeatedly in the lead-up to matches. Some Pakistani fans found it harder to attend games in their own country than foreign visitors who booked through official ICC channels. This is an administrative failure the PCB must own and correct.

**Media facilities.** Several accredited journalists raised concerns about working conditions, specifically cramped press boxes, inconsistent internet connectivity in media centres, and accreditation bottlenecks on match days. For a tournament broadcast to hundreds of millions globally, the experience of the working press affects coverage quality. These are fixable problems that should not recur.

**City spread and scheduling.** Using Lahore, Karachi, and Rawalpindi spread the event thin. No single city built the sustained atmosphere a focused tournament requires. Compare this with how South Africa concentrated World Cup energy in a handful of hubs during the 2007 edition. Pakistan has strong enough venues to anchor a tournament more tightly.

## What This Means Going Forward

Pakistan is reportedly in the frame for co-hosting future ICC events, including potential joint bids involving other Asian boards. The Champions Trophy experience is both a credential and a cautionary tale. The credential: Pakistan can host international cricket safely, with enthusiastic crowds and serviceable infrastructure. The caution: administrative and diplomatic preparation must be sharper, the ticketing operation overhauled, and media facilities upgraded to genuine international standard.

The PCB chairman and his board need to treat this tournament as a detailed audit rather than a victory lap. The wins are real. So are the gaps.

## What to Watch Next

The ICC's internal evaluation of the tournament, including broadcaster feedback and team reports, will carry more weight than public statements. Watch whether Pakistan secures a confirmed co-hosting role in the 2031 World Cup cycle — that outcome will reveal how the game's administrators genuinely rate what was delivered here. Domestically, the PCB must demonstrate it has absorbed the ticketing and media lessons before the next PSL season, which serves as the annual dress rehearsal for exactly this kind of large-scale event management.

Pakistan cricket earned something important by pulling this off at all. Now it has to prove that was the floor, not the ceiling.
