Pakistan's first major ICC tournament on home soil since 1996 carried enormous symbolic weight, and by most measures the country delivered a creditable debut on the modern hosting stage. But creditable is not the same as flawless, and a clear-eyed review of the Champions Trophy 2025 reveals a mixed scorecard that will shape how the PCB pitches for future events.

## The Real Story: A Nation Auditioning for the Future

This tournament was never just about cricket. It was Pakistan making a sustained argument to the ICC, to broadcasters, and to the wider cricketing world that it can host elite events safely, professionally, and profitably. The political dimension, especially the hybrid model forced upon the PCB after India refused to travel, already cost Pakistan matches it should have hosted. Against that backdrop, everything the country did with the games it did host carried extra scrutiny.

The argument, on balance, was partially won.

## What Pakistan Got Right

**The pitches and outfields.** Lahore's Gaddafi Stadium and Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium produced results. Tracks were competitive without being embarrassing, offering something to seamers early and to batters who survived the new ball. The groundstaff deserved considerable credit for surfaces that held up through a congested schedule.

**Security and logistics.** The feared operational nightmare did not materialise. Accredited media, team delegations, and match officials moved without the chaos that critics predicted. Security arrangements were visible but not oppressive. The PCB and the government agencies involved clearly prepared seriously for this element.

**Crowd atmosphere.** Pakistani supporters turned out with genuine passion. Gaddafi Stadium in particular produced an atmosphere that reminded neutral observers why this country belongs on the global stage. Full houses, vocal crowds, and colourful stands were a consistent feature.

**Broadcasting infrastructure.** Feeds were largely clean and professional. Commentary and production quality met ICC standards without notable embarrassment, which was not guaranteed given how long Pakistan had been out of this game.

## What Pakistan Got Wrong

**Ticketing chaos.** This was the most damaging failure. Reports of long queues, confused online systems, and supporters unable to enter grounds they had paid to enter were widespread. For an event of this scale, ticketing logistics should have been resolved months in advance through stress-tested systems. The PCB appeared to underestimate demand in some cities while struggling to convert that demand into smooth matchday access. This is not a minor administrative hiccup. It is the fan experience, and it is what casual supporters remember.

**Venue limitations outside Lahore.** Rawalpindi served its purpose, but gaps in hospitality infrastructure, media facilities, and general venue amenity were evident. The corporate and media experience at a Champions Trophy needs to match what broadcasters and sponsors are accustomed to in Dubai, London, or Melbourne. It did not always do so.

**The hybrid model shadow.** Pakistan cannot be fully blamed for a geopolitical dispute it did not start, but the resulting arrangement meant the final and India's matches moved to Dubai. The PCB allowed itself to be manoeuvred into a position where the tournament's highest-profile fixture took place outside its borders. Whether that was avoidable is debatable. What is not debatable is that it undermined the core narrative of Pakistan as a complete host nation.

**Communication with the public.** Schedule changes, venue updates, and logistical information could have been managed more transparently. Supporters often found out about changes through social media rumour before any official communication arrived.

## What This Means Going Forward

The PCB now has a genuine template, both for what works and what needs urgent fixing. If Pakistan is to pursue co-hosting rights for future events, including a potential World Cup role, the ticketing and hospitality deficiencies must be addressed structurally, not patched event by event. International fans and touring broadcasters make decisions about future investment based on this kind of experience.

Naseem Shah bowling to a packed Gaddafi is the image Pakistan wants the world to carry. An angry supporter locked outside a sold-out match he paid for is not.

## What to Watch Next

The ICC will conduct its own host assessment, and its findings, even if not published publicly, will influence future hosting allocations. Watch how aggressively the PCB lobbies for the 2031 World Cup co-hosting role and whether it publicly commits to ticketing and infrastructure reforms as part of that bid. Also watch whether the hybrid model becomes entrenched for future ICC events involving India and Pakistan, because that fight is far from over and Pakistan's negotiating position depends heavily on proving it can host without caveats.
